Web novels are not one shelf. The same reader can hold a cosy romance, a
serial that turns violent in chapter forty, and a translated epic with a
content warning the size of a paragraph. Glyph does not decide what is
appropriate for you; it lets you say what you want to see.

That switch is the whole feature, and it needed a post because it is easy to
misread as something else.

## What the rating actually is

A content rating is a label on a source, set by whoever publishes it and
visible before you install anything. It is not a review, not a score, and not a
judgement about whether a book is good.

- **Everyone** — nothing you would not see on a broadcast network.
- **Teen** — violence, language, or themes aimed at older readers.
- **Mature** — explicit content, plainly labelled.

If a repository does not declare a rating, Glyph treats the source as
unlabelled and shows it that way. Unlabelled is not a synonym for "safe".

## Why the label lives with the source

The app could try to detect content, and it will not. Detection means reading
what you read, and Glyph has no business doing that. The label travels with the
extension instead, which keeps the judgement on the side of the person who
wrote the scraper and takes it out of your reading session entirely.

> Nothing about your reading is uploaded to decide a rating. The switch filters
> a list of sources; it does not inspect a single chapter.

## Turning it on

The filter is in Settings → Content, and it applies to the Browse tab and to
search results. Installed sources stay installed — switching the filter off the
next day brings them back, exactly as they were.
