Every reader app makes the same choice early: ship a catalogue, or ship
nothing. Glyph ships nothing. The app is a browser built for reading, and the
sources come from the people who use it.

That decision is behind almost every other thing about the app.

## What it means in practice

- The Browse tab is empty until you install a source, and then it is yours.
- Nobody at Glyph decides which novels exist, which are promoted, or which are
  removed.
- The extension format is open, so the ecosystem does not depend on one app
  staying in one store.

## What it costs

An empty app is a worse first five minutes than one with a shelf. It also means
support questions start at "where do I find a repository" rather than "why is
this chapter broken", and that the documentation has to carry a load the
interface cannot.

> A catalogue is a liability dressed as a feature. The moment it exists,
> someone is responsible for what is in it, and the reading stops being
> between you and the site you chose.

## Where the sources come from

Community repositories, shared as URLs. Some are maintained by one person for
one site; some cover a dozen novel sites at once. The SDK and the WIT contract
are public, and the guides walk from a first scraper to a published repository.
